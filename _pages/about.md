### Children are growing up in a world increasingly shaped by artificial intelligence and social robots. 

AI-powered technologies are becoming part of children's everyday lives through educational tools, conversational agents, recommender systems, social media platforms, and social robots. These technologies create new opportunities for learning, participation, creativity, and social interaction, while also raising important questions about children's development, wellbeing, and future relationship with intelligent systems.

The Child-AI project investigates how interactions with AI systems and social robots shape children's development across three interconnected areas: AI literacy, socio-emotional development, and cognitive skills and wellbeing. Particular attention is given to how children understand, trust, learn from, and make decisions with AI technologies.

The project brings together researchers from developmental psychology, cognitive science, social robotics, education, human-computer interaction, ethics, and AI policy to develop a comprehensive understanding of children's experiences with AI. Through interdisciplinary research, Child-AI seeks to identify both the opportunities and risks associated with children's interactions with intelligent technologies.

By combining empirical studies, participatory design approaches, and collaboration with educators, families, policymakers, and technology developers, Child-AI aims to generate evidence-based recommendations for the ethical design and implementation of AI systems that support children's learning, development, wellbeing, and future participation in society.


<h3>Research Themes</h3>

AI Literacy

How children understand, interpret, and develop knowledge about AI systems, including how they learn to critically evaluate AI-generated information and recommendations.

Socio-Emotional Development

How interactions with AI systems and social robots influence children's social relationships, empathy, trust, collaboration, and emotional development.

Cognitive Skills and Wellbeing

How AI technologies affect children's critical thinking, decision-making, health literacy, wellbeing, and other higher-order cognitive skills.
